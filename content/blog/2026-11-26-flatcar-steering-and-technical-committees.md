Flatcar is forming its first Steering Committee and Technical Committee, and volunteering is open now until Wednesday 9 December.
 
In October, the Flatcar maintainers voted to adopt charters for both committees: a [Steering Committee](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/committees/steering-committee/charter.md) and a [Technical Committee](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/committees/technical-committee/charter.md). In November, they voted on how to get them started, and welcomed our first Members through a new contributor ladder. It's one of the biggest changes to how Flatcar is run since the project began, and an important step on our path to CNCF graduation.
 
## Why we did this
 
Flatcar has always been governed by a flat group of maintainers, and that has served us well. As the project and its community have grown, though, some questions don't fit neatly into any one subgroup: who looks after project-wide assets, how we make architectural decisions that touch many repositories, and how someone raises a concern when they feel stuck.
 
The new committees give those questions a clear home, while keeping to the [values in our governance](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/governance.md#values): openness, fairness, inclusivity, and community over any single company.
 
## What each committee does
 
The **Steering Committee** looks after the non-technical side of the project. That includes governance and community structure, charters for project-wide groups, project assets such as the GitHub organisation, website and social accounts, and Flatcar's relationship with the CNCF.
 
The **Technical Committee** looks after Flatcar's technical health and direction. That includes architecture, project-wide technical standards, the process for technical proposals, and technical questions that cross more than one subgroup.
 
Together, the two committees set Flatcar's direction, each in its own area. Day-to-day work stays with the maintainers and subgroups: reviewing and merging, running releases, CI and tooling all carry on exactly as before.
 
## How to volunteer
 
Both committees start as bootstrap committees, as Kubernetes did in 2017. Instead of a full election, eligible people volunteer, and everyone who volunteers and isn't vetoed joins.
 
You can volunteer if you're a Flatcar maintainer, a [Member](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/MEMBERS.md), or someone who works for or represents an organisation listed in [ADOPTERS.md](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/ADOPTERS.md). That means the people who run Flatcar in production get a voice too.
 
To volunteer, leave a short comment on the issue for the committee you'd like to join by **Wednesday 9 December at 23:59 PST**:
 
* [Volunteer for the Steering Committee](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/issues/2514)
* [Volunteer for the Technical Committee](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/issues/2515)
You can sit on one committee, not both. If you contribute regularly but aren't a Member yet, you can become one through the [contributor ladder](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/CONTRIBUTOR_LADDER.md). Member PRs stay open for at least a week, so if you'd like to volunteer this time, open yours by Wednesday 2 December.
 
Here's what happens next:
 
* **10 December:** we post the list of volunteers on each issue.
* **10 to 16 December:** veto week. The maintainers confirm adopter listings and can raise any concerns.
* **17 December:** both committees are seated.
The founding committees serve for one year. Their first job is to set the rules for future terms, including how elections will work, with a public comment period before anything is adopted.
 
## No single company in charge
 
We want Flatcar's governance to be genuinely multi-company, so a few rules are built in from the start:
 
* In the founding term, no single company can hold more than 40% of the seats on the Steering Committee, or more than four seats on the Technical Committee, counting its subsidiaries and affiliates. The founding committees will decide the limits for later terms.
* Every committee decision needs yes votes from members of at least two different companies, again counting subsidiaries and affiliates as one company.
* Once it's seated, the Steering Committee approves changes to our governance, so maintainers don't have to vote on every one.
* Nobody can sit on both committees at once.
## Meet our first Members
 
Our governance has always promised "a clear path up the contributor ladder", and now it's written down. The [contributor ladder](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/CONTRIBUTOR_LADDER.md) adds a **Member** role between Contributor and Maintainer, for people who take part in Flatcar regularly. Contributions don't have to be code: docs, triage, answering questions, talks, blog posts, events, design and release work all count. New Members are sponsored by two maintainers, at least one of whom works for a different company.
 
A warm welcome to our first two [Members](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/MEMBERS.md):
 
* **Logan Blyth** from Hedgehog, one of our adopters, who has been helping with community and graduation work
* **Daniel Szot** from Microsoft, who has been automating our security triage
If you think you, or someone you know, should be a Member, the [ladder](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/CONTRIBUTOR_LADDER.md) explains how to join.
 
## A clearer adopters list
 
[ADOPTERS.md](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/ADOPTERS.md) now follows the CNCF's definition of a direct adopter, so it's clear who should be listed. If your organisation runs Flatcar and isn't on the list yet, we'd love to add you.
 
## None of this is set in stone
 
Like our code, our governance will keep evolving. When Kubernetes started its governance, its [bootstrap committee wrote](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2017/10/Kubernetes-Community-Steering-Committee-Election-Results/) that "we do not believe that we will get it right the first time, or possibly ever", and we feel the same. Changes go through issues and pull requests, just like code.
