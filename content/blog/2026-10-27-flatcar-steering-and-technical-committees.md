On Friday 16 October 2026, the Flatcar maintainers voted to adopt charters for two new governing bodies: a [Steering Committee](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/committees/steering-committee/charter.md) and a [Technical Committee](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/committees/technical-committee/charter.md). They give the project structured leadership, with clear owners for its direction and built-in limits on how many members can come from one company.

## Why we did this

Flatcar has been run as a flat hierarchy of maintainers, with the Maintainer Council as its governing body. The committees add something that structure didn't have: clear rules that keep governance balanced across companies.

Our [values](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/blob/main/governance.md#values) already put community over product or company, and say that each contributor takes part in the project as an individual. The committee charters put that into practice by limiting how many people from one company can sit on each committee:

- **Steering Committee:** no more than two members from the same company in the founding term. The charter currently sets a tighter limit of one seat per company from the second term onward.
- **Technical Committee:** a deliberately looser limit of no more than four members from the same company, so there is always at least one independent voice on technical governance while our contributor base is still concentrated. A tighter limit for later terms is part of the committee's first-year work.

The committees also give project-wide questions a clear home: who looks after shared assets like the GitHub organisation and website, how architectural decisions that touch many repositories get made, and where to turn when a disagreement is stuck.

## What each committee does

The **Steering Committee** owns the non-technical direction of the project: its vision, mission and values, its governance and community structure, charters for project-wide groups such as working groups and subgroups, project assets like the GitHub organisation, websites, mailing lists and social accounts, and Flatcar's relationship with the CNCF. It's also the final escalation point for non-technical disputes.

The **Technical Committee** owns Flatcar's technical direction and architecture: project-wide technical standards, the process for technical proposals and design reviews, and technical questions that cross more than one repository or subgroup.

Day-to-day work stays with the maintainers and their subgroups: reviewing and merging pull requests, running releases, triaging issues, and running CI and tooling all carry on through the normal process, with no committee approval needed. The committees step in when a matter is project-wide or cross-cutting, or when it's escalated to them.

## How the first year works

Both committees start with a one-year founding term, using the starting rules set out in their charters. None of this is set in stone: the founding committees will shape the rules for their second term onward, including the number of seats, who can stand and vote, how members are chosen, term limits and company representation, and the charters can keep evolving as the project grows. The committees will publish a first full draft around nine months in and open a public comment period before anything is adopted.

Nobody can sit on both committees at the same time.

## Get involved

Maintainers and recognised Contributors are automatically eligible to stand for either committee. We're still defining the recognised Contributor role in [#2216](https://github.com/flatcar/Flatcar/issues/2216). Anyone else who has made multiple meaningful contributions to Flatcar in the last 12 months, technical or not, can also stand after a short check by the maintainers. That check looks at what you've contributed, not your job title or employer. Responsibility in Flatcar is earned through participation, and this is one more path into leadership.

If that sounds like you, we'd love for you to take part. And if someone comes to mind who'd do a great job, please encourage them too.

We'll share how to get involved, along with the dates, in early November. Keep an eye on [Discord](https://discord.gg/PMYjFUsJyq), our Slack channels and the mailing list, or come along to [Office Hours](https://calendar.google.com/calendar/u/0/embed?src=c_ii991mqrpta9en8o7ofd4v19g4@group.calendar.google.com).
